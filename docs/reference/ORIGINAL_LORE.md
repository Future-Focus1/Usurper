# USURPER

## The Crown Is Empty

For almost forty years, **King Erol II of House Valen** ruled the Kingdom of Nezrel.

He was not universally loved, but he was respected. Under Erol, the borders remained secure, the roads became safer, trade flourished and the feuding houses of Nezrel were kept under control.

Then the King disappeared.

For three weeks Erol did not attend court.

Petitions went unanswered. Council meetings were postponed. Foreign ambassadors were refused entry to the royal chambers. Servants whispered that the King had become sick. Others claimed he had gone mad. Some insisted that he was already dead.

Queen Mira repeatedly assured the court that her husband required only rest.

Then, shortly before sunrise on the twenty-third day of his absence, the bells of **Castle Caerlyn** began to ring.

King Erol was dead.

He was seventy years old.

The official declaration stated that the King had died peacefully after a brief illness.

Few believed it.

There was no public viewing of the body.

The royal physicians were forbidden from speaking about his final days.

Several servants who attended the King's chambers disappeared from court shortly afterwards.

And most troubling of all:

**King Erol left no publicly acknowledged heir.**

Within hours, the question whispered throughout Nezrel was no longer how the King had died.

It was:

**Who will wear the crown?**

---

# THE KINGDOM OF NEZREL

Nezrel is an old kingdom built from territories that were once independent tribes, city-states and petty kingdoms.

The monarchy has existed for roughly six hundred years, although the borders of Nezrel have changed many times.

The kingdom is wealthy enough to be powerful but not powerful enough to be secure.

To the north lie mountains and iron-rich lands controlled by ancient noble houses.

To the east stand fertile plains and great estates producing wheat, horses and cattle.

The southern coast contains Nezrel's largest ports and merchant cities.

The west is dominated by forests, ruined fortresses and isolated communities where loyalty to the Crown has always been weak.

At the kingdom's centre sits the capital:

## Caerlyn

Caerlyn is the political and symbolic heart of Nezrel.

The city grew around **Castle Caerlyn**, a massive fortress built upon a rocky hill overlooking the River Vey.

Nearly sixty thousand people live within its walls and surrounding districts.

The wealthy reside around the King's Road and Temple Quarter. Merchants dominate the riverside. Craftsmen, labourers and soldiers fill the sprawling districts beneath the castle.

Caerlyn is where kings are crowned.

And where they are usually killed.

Throughout Nezrel's history, more monarchs have died violently inside Caerlyn than on any battlefield.

The saying among commoners is:

> "A king fears the battlefield. A wise king fears his bedroom."

---

# THE CROWN OF NEZREL

The monarchy is hereditary, but Nezrel has never possessed a perfectly defined law of succession.

Tradition carries almost as much weight as written law.

The accepted order generally favours legitimate sons, followed by brothers and their descendants.

But exceptions have occurred.

The most famous came more than two centuries ago when **Queen Aria Valen** took the throne after the deaths of her father and two brothers.

Her reign lasted twenty-seven years.

She became one of Nezrel's most celebrated rulers.

Yet rather than establishing female succession permanently, Aria's reign became regarded as an extraordinary exception.

The nobles still argue over what it means.

Supporters of Queen Mira say Aria proved that the Crown recognises ability rather than sex.

Opponents say Aria inherited royal blood directly from her father, whereas Mira merely married into the dynasty.

This seemingly obscure distinction may decide whether Nezrel accepts its second ruling queen.

---

# THE EMPTY THRONE

Traditionally the monarch is crowned by the **High Keeper of the Flame** inside the Hall of Kings.

However, a coronation requires recognition from at least three pillars of the kingdom:

**The Crown**, represented by possession of Caerlyn and the royal regalia.

**The Great Houses**, represented through the Council of Lords.

**The Flame**, represented by Nezrel's dominant religious institution.

A claimant possessing all three is effectively uncontested.

A claimant possessing two can probably rule.

A claimant possessing only one is likely to face civil war.

This makes power in Nezrel something that must be constructed rather than simply inherited.

---

# THE FOUR CLAIMANTS

## QUEEN MIRA VALEN

**Age: 54**

Queen Mira has lived inside Castle Caerlyn for thirty-one years.

She understands its corridors, servants, nobles, secrets and rivalries better than perhaps anyone alive.

Born Mira Edevane, she came from a relatively minor noble family and married Erol long before he became king.

Their marriage was considered unusually affectionate during its early years.

But there was one great failure.

They never produced a surviving child.

Mira suffered several pregnancies, none of which resulted in a living heir.

For decades, the question of succession followed her everywhere.

Some blamed Erol.

Most blamed Mira.

The Queen gradually became harder, more private and considerably more politically capable.

By the final decade of Erol's reign, foreign diplomats often observed that petitions supposedly presented to the King appeared to bear Mira's influence.

She never officially ruled.

But many believed she governed Nezrel whenever Erol lost interest in doing so.

### Mira's Claim

Mira's argument is simple.

She has ruled beside Erol for thirty-one years.

She knows the kingdom.

She understands its finances, alliances, enemies and court.

And Nezrel has accepted a queen before.

Why should the death of her husband suddenly make her less capable than she was the day before?

### Mira's Greatest Strength

She begins inside Castle Caerlyn.

She controls access to the royal apartments, much of the treasury, the King's correspondence and the royal seal.

More importantly, she commands the **Queen's Guard**.

### Mira's Greatest Weakness

Many nobles regard the idea of Queen Mira ruling alone as unacceptable.

Others believe she murdered her husband.

And somewhere inside Caerlyn may exist evidence proving that Erol never intended her to inherit the Crown.

### Mira's Secret

Mira knows far more about Erol's death than she has admitted.

Possibly everything.

---

# THE QUEEN'S GUARD

The Queen's Guard numbers only **forty-eight soldiers**.

They are not an army.

They are something more dangerous.

Every member is personally selected and extensively trained in swordsmanship, escort, investigation and palace security.

They guard the royal family rather than the kingdom.

Their loyalty traditionally belongs to the reigning queen, making them one of the few military institutions Mira controls directly.

Their current commander is **Captain Garran Vale**, a quiet, disciplined veteran who once served beneath Ethan.

Garran respects Mira.

But he worshipped Ethan.

If the King's brother returned to Caerlyn, Garran's loyalty could become dangerously divided.

---

# ETHAN VALEN

**Age: 61**

Once there were few men more admired in Nezrel than Prince Ethan Valen.

Erol was born to rule.

Ethan was born to serve.

As a young man he rejected the ceremonial life expected of a prince and joined the royal army.

Eventually he became commander of the Queen's Guard.

For eleven years Ethan travelled throughout Nezrel suppressing banditry, settling disputes, escorting royal officials and occasionally confronting rebellious nobles.

He developed a reputation for fairness.

Unlike many nobles, Ethan frequently ate with soldiers and spoke directly with commoners.

Stories about him became popular throughout the kingdom.

Then everything collapsed.

Six years ago Queen Mira accused Ethan of entering her chambers and forcing himself upon her.

Mira claimed Ethan had pursued her for years and finally acted when Erol was away hunting.

Ethan denied everything.

There were no witnesses.

Erol nevertheless believed his wife.

The King offered his brother two choices.

Execution.

Or permanent exile.

Ethan chose exile.

He surrendered his sword, abandoned his titles and disappeared.

For six years he has lived as a traveller, labourer and occasional hired guard under false names.

He has made no attempt to reclaim his position.

Until Erol dies.

### Ethan's Claim

By traditional Nezreli succession, Ethan may possess the strongest recognised legal claim.

Erol has no acknowledged legitimate children.

Ethan is his younger brother.

To many people the matter should end there.

### Ethan's Greatest Strength

The people remember him.

Soldiers remember him.

And many members of the Queen's Guard were trained by him.

If Ethan openly returned, he could become enormously popular almost overnight.

### Ethan's Greatest Weakness

The accusation against him never disappeared.

Supporting Ethan requires deciding whether Queen Mira lied about being assaulted.

For many nobles, particularly those close to Mira, that makes him unacceptable.

### Ethan's Secret

Ethan knows something that has never been revealed publicly.

On the night Mira claimed he attacked her, Ethan **was inside the Queen's chambers**.

He simply refuses to explain why.

---

# WHAT REALLY HAPPENED BETWEEN ETHAN AND MIRA

Years before the accusation, Ethan discovered evidence suggesting Erol had fathered an illegitimate child.

Mira already knew.

She summoned Ethan privately because she feared Erol intended to acknowledge the boy.

Their argument became vicious.

Mira demanded Ethan swear that if Erol legitimised the child, Ethan would support her attempt to prevent it.

Ethan refused.

He believed the boy should not be punished for the circumstances of his birth.

A servant unexpectedly approached the chambers.

Mira panicked.

Being discovered alone with her husband's brother late at night could itself create scandal.

What happened afterwards remains disputed even between the two of them.

Mira claims Ethan grabbed her during the argument.

Ethan claims he restrained her after she attempted to strike him.

Mira later told Erol that Ethan had attacked her sexually.

Whether this was a deliberate lie, a distorted version of an angry encounter, or something more complicated can remain partially dependent upon discoveries made during the game.

What matters politically is that both know the official story is incomplete.

---

# ARLO

**Age: 24**

Arlo has never slept beneath a royal roof.

He lives almost three hundred miles from Caerlyn in the eastern estate of **House Maren**.

Officially, Arlo is nobody.

His mother, Lysa, worked briefly as a servant at a royal hunting lodge.

She became pregnant shortly after Erol stayed there.

She refused to identify the father.

Lysa died giving birth.

The infant was taken in by **Lord Tomas Maren** and his wife Lady Evelyne.

They raised Arlo alongside their own children.

Not quite a servant.

Not quite family.

He learned to read, ride and fight, but possesses no title.

Arlo now manages much of House Maren's prized stable.

He is particularly gifted with horses and is known throughout the region as an exceptional rider.

Then there are the payments.

For twenty-three years, House Maren received an annual payment from an obscure account controlled by the royal household.

The payments ended the month Erol died.

Lord Tomas has never explained them.

### Arlo's Claim

If Arlo can prove Erol was his father, everything changes.

An acknowledged illegitimate son would not automatically inherit.

But a **legitimised** son could outrank Ethan.

And rumours suggest Erol had been quietly investigating precisely how such legitimisation might be accomplished.

### Arlo's Greatest Strength

Arlo is almost completely untouched by the corruption of Caerlyn.

To ordinary people he could become something extraordinary:

A king who actually knows how they live.

### Arlo's Greatest Weakness

He has no army.

No fortune.

No political experience.

No recognised name.

At the beginning of the game, half the kingdom would laugh at the suggestion that a stable boy could become king.

### Arlo's Secret

Arlo met Erol.

Not once.

Many times.

Beginning when Arlo was thirteen, an elderly nobleman calling himself **Master Ren** occasionally visited House Maren.

He taught Arlo history, politics and chess.

Arlo discovered the man's identity years later.

Neither ever acknowledged their relationship aloud.

Their final meeting occurred approximately two months before Erol disappeared from court.

Before leaving, Erol told him:

> "There may come a day when someone tells you that you were born with nothing. Do not believe them."

---

# LORD ALISTAIR STORM

**Age: 46**

House Storm existed before Nezrel.

Or at least that is what the Storms claim.

For more than four hundred years they have ruled the northern territory known as **The Reach of Storms**, where mountains, forests and iron mines make invasion extremely difficult.

Their ancestral fortress, **Stormhold**, has never been captured.

Lord Alistair inherited the North at seventeen after his father died fighting raiders beyond the mountains.

He has ruled it ever since.

Efficiently.

Ruthlessly.

Successfully.

Banditry is rare.

Taxes are collected.

Roads are maintained.

Criminals fear his magistrates.

His soldiers are among the best equipped in Nezrel.

Alistair believes deeply in order.

He also believes most people are incapable of maintaining it.

### Alistair's Claim

Legally, his claim is weak.

Politically, it is terrifying.

Alistair argues that Nezrel is facing an unprecedented succession crisis.

Mira has no royal blood.

Ethan is a disgraced exile.

Arlo is an unproven bastard.

Therefore the throne must pass to someone capable of defending the kingdom.

Alistair has publicly offered Mira a solution.

Marry him.

Crown him King.

Unite royal legitimacy with northern military power.

If she refuses, he intends to march south.

### Alistair's Greatest Strength

Alistair begins with something none of the others possess.

An army.

Nearly eight thousand soldiers answer directly to House Storm.

Several northern houses will provide thousands more.

### Alistair's Greatest Weakness

Almost everyone outside the North knows what he is doing.

It is conquest disguised as necessity.

If the other claimants unite against him, even temporarily, his advantage disappears.

### Alistair's Secret

Alistair knew the King was dying before almost anyone else.

Someone inside Castle Caerlyn had been corresponding with him for months.

And Alistair has destroyed every letter.

Except one.

---

# THE DEAD KING

Erol's public image was that of a stubborn but dependable monarch.

The private man was considerably more complicated.

He loved Mira.

He loved Ethan.

And in his own distant way, he loved Arlo.

Those three relationships became increasingly impossible to reconcile.

During his final years Erol became obsessed with one question:

**What happens after me?**

He knew Mira would never willingly surrender power.

He suspected Ethan might refuse the throne even if offered it.

And he feared revealing Arlo could destabilise the kingdom.

So Erol did what he had done throughout his reign.

He delayed making a decision.

Until illness removed the choice.

---

# THE KING'S ILLNESS

Approximately six months before his death, Erol began experiencing weakness in his hands.

Then headaches.

Then periods of confusion.

Eventually he began forgetting conversations.

Royal physician **Master Oren Pell** concluded that the King was suffering from an incurable disease affecting the brain.

Erol ordered Pell to tell nobody.

Not the Council.

Not Ethan.

Not the kingdom.

Only Mira knew.

As the disease progressed, Erol became paranoid and unpredictable.

Some days he appeared entirely normal.

Others he accused lifelong servants of treason.

He began writing contradictory instructions regarding the succession.

One document favoured Ethan.

Another discussed legitimising Arlo.

Another granted Mira extraordinary emergency powers.

This creates an enormous problem.

Several authentic documents exist expressing entirely different final wishes.

Each claimant can therefore discover evidence supporting them.

---

# THE NIGHT KING EROL DIED

The truth should form the central mystery of Usurper.

Erol's final night involved only four known people.

Queen Mira.

Master Oren Pell.

A servant named **Nessa Grey**.

And the King himself.

By midnight, Erol was in terrible pain.

Pell gave him medicine.

It did little.

Several hours later Erol begged Mira not to allow him to become the helpless, confused man his illness would eventually create.

Shortly before dawn, Mira dismissed Pell.

Nessa claims she later saw Mira carrying the King's medicine.

Erol was dead within the hour.

The problem is that Nessa disappeared before she could give formal testimony.

Master Pell refuses to state whether the amount of medicine found in Erol's body could have killed him.

And Mira refuses to discuss the King's final words.

The player gradually discovers something extraordinary.

**Erol may have asked Mira to kill him.**

Whether she actually did so remains difficult to prove.

Mira herself may not even describe it that way.

She would say:

> "My husband asked me for mercy. I gave it to him."

Her enemies would use a simpler word.

Murder.

---

# THE MISSING TESTAMENT

The night before Erol died, he dictated one final document.

It has disappeared.

Everyone wants it.

Nobody knows exactly what it contained.

Rumours claim it named a successor.

Others say Erol dissolved the traditional succession entirely and demanded that the Great Houses choose the next monarch.

One person knows more.

Nessa Grey.

Finding her can become one of the largest storylines in the game.

But even Nessa never read the entire document.

She only remembers its final line:

> "The Crown must belong not to the one born nearest it, but to the one who can keep Nezrel whole."

That sentence can support almost anyone.

Which may have been Erol's intention.

---

# THE GREAT HOUSES

Nezrel's throne cannot survive on royal authority alone.

Several houses effectively control parts of the kingdom.

## House Storm

Northern military powerhouse.

Leader: **Lord Alistair Storm**

Resources: Iron, timber, professional soldiers.

Reputation: Disciplined, proud, severe.

Motto:

**"We Do Not Bend."**

---

## House Maren

Wealthy eastern landowners and horse breeders.

Leader: **Lord Tomas Maren**

Resources: Horses, farmland, money.

Reputation: Honourable, cautious, politically neutral.

Secret importance: Raised Arlo.

Motto:

**"What We Sow Endures."**

---

## House Veyne

Controls much of Nezrel's southern coastline.

Leader: **Lady Celene Veyne**

Resources: Ships, ports, trade.

Celene cares little who becomes monarch provided trade remains uninterrupted.

She may become one of the game's most important kingmakers.

Motto:

**"All Rivers Reach Us."**

---

## House Harrow

An ancient western family whose lands have declined economically.

Leader: **Lord Osric Harrow**

Resources: Large population but little money.

Harrow despises House Storm and would support almost anyone preventing Alistair becoming king.

Motto:

**"Remember the Old Blood."**

---

## House Edevane

Queen Mira's birth family.

Leader: **Lord Edric Edevane**, Mira's nephew.

Resources: Wealth, court connections.

The family owes almost everything to Mira's marriage.

If she falls, they may fall with her.

Motto:

**"Wisdom Before Steel."**

---

# THE FAITH OF THE FLAME

Most people in Nezrel follow a religion commonly called **The Flame**.

Its teachings claim that every person carries a spark of the divine fire.

Kings are not considered gods.

Instead the monarch is believed to be the **Keeper of the Hearth**, responsible for preserving peace so the people's flame may survive.

Coronations therefore require the High Keeper to light the Crown Candle from the ancient flame inside Caerlyn's Great Temple.

The current High Keeper is **Father Aldren Veil**.

He is seventy-eight.

Intelligent.

Politically cautious.

And increasingly worried that supporting the wrong monarch could destroy the institution he has spent his life protecting.

The Flame's scriptures never explicitly prohibit female rulers.

Queen Aria's reign proves this.

But many provincial priests teach that leadership of the household belongs to men.

Mira therefore faces religious opposition without the Church formally condemning her.

---

# QUEEN ARIA

Aria should loom over the entire story despite having died more than two centuries earlier.

She was twenty-six when she took the throne after her brothers died during a plague.

Several lords rebelled rather than recognise her.

Aria defeated them.

She ruled for twenty-seven years and transformed the kingdom.

Roads were built.

Judicial courts were standardised.

Private noble armies were restricted.

She became known as:

**Aria the First Flame.**

Mira deliberately invokes Aria's legacy.

Her enemies respond by pointing out that Aria was the legitimate daughter of a king.

There is also a dangerous historical rumour.

Aria may not actually have been the king's biological daughter.

If evidence of this survives somewhere, it could completely transform the debate around legitimacy.

---

# THE COUNCIL OF NINE

The King traditionally receives advice from nine senior figures known collectively as **the King's Council**.

With Erol dead, they have become immensely powerful.

Among them are:

**Chancellor Lucan Rook**, keeper of royal law and records. He believes stability matters more than legitimacy.

**Marshal Bren Tal**, commander of the royal army. He refuses to deploy his soldiers until a lawful monarch is established.

**Lady Celene Veyne**, representing southern trade interests.

**Master Oren Pell**, the royal physician, whose knowledge of Erol's death makes him extremely dangerous.

**Father Aldren Veil**, High Keeper of the Flame.

The remaining council seats can become recruitable allies, rivals or potential traitors.

Whoever controls the Council controls the machinery of government.

---

# THE ROYAL ARMY

The death of the King creates a strange military situation.

Nezrel possesses around twenty thousand royal soldiers.

But they have sworn loyalty to **the Crown**, not Mira personally.

Marshal Bren refuses to declare for any claimant until succession is established.

This means the largest army in Nezrel begins the game essentially neutral.

Every claimant therefore has a reason to court Bren.

Mira can argue that she represents continuity.

Ethan can invoke royal blood.

Arlo could eventually present proof of his parentage.

Alistair can simply remind Bren what happens if the army remains neutral while Nezrel collapses.

---

# THE COMMON PEOPLE

The population does not speak with one voice.

Caerlyn fears war more than anything.

Northern communities admire Alistair's strength.

Veterans remember Ethan.

The rural east gradually becomes fascinated by rumours of a hidden royal son.

Women in the cities increasingly speak of Mira becoming another Queen Aria.

These opinions should change dynamically throughout the game.

A claimant can therefore win battles but lose legitimacy.

Or gain enormous popularity while possessing very little military power.

---

# THE FOUR OPENINGS

Each campaign should begin completely differently.

## Playing Queen Mira

The player begins inside Castle Caerlyn on the morning Erol's death is announced.

You already possess power.

Your problem is keeping it.

Your first hours involve controlling information, securing the treasury, preventing the Council from declaring Ethan king and deciding what should happen to Erol's body.

You know secrets none of the other characters know.

But you also immediately understand how vulnerable you are.

Your campaign feels like political intrigue.

---

## Playing Ethan

The player begins far from Caerlyn.

You are living under another name when travellers arrive with news that the King is dead.

You possess almost nothing.

An old sword.

A horse.

A few coins.

And a name you have not spoken publicly for six years.

Your first major decision is whether to reveal yourself.

Your campaign feels like the return of a forgotten hero who may gradually become something very different.

---

## Playing Arlo

You begin in a stable.

You do not even initially consider yourself a claimant.

Then Lord Tomas asks you to come inside.

Waiting on the table is a sealed letter delivered shortly before the King's death.

It contains Erol's royal seal.

Your campaign begins as discovery.

You gradually uncover who you are before deciding whether that gives you any right to rule.

---

## Playing Alistair

Your campaign begins at Stormhold.

You already command land, wealth and soldiers.

Messengers arrive confirming Erol's death.

Your generals await instructions.

Your first decision is whether to march immediately or attempt diplomacy.

Alistair begins closest to conquering the kingdom militarily.

But conquest may be the easiest part.

Getting Nezrel to accept him afterwards is much harder.

---

# THE CENTRAL THEME

Usurper should continually ask one question:

**What actually makes someone a rightful ruler?**

Blood?

Law?

Popular support?

Military strength?

Competence?

Religion?

The wishes of the previous king?

Every claimant embodies a different answer.

Mira represents **experience and continuity**.

Ethan represents **tradition and reputation**.

Arlo represents **blood and possibility**.

Alistair represents **strength and order**.

But the player can corrupt every philosophy.

Mira can become a calculating tyrant.

Ethan can return as a vengeful conqueror.

Arlo can become obsessed with proving that he deserves something he never previously wanted.

Alistair can surprisingly become the ruler who creates the most stable kingdom.

Likewise, each can become genuinely admirable.

The identity of the ruler matters.

But **what the player does to obtain the throne should matter far more.**

---

# LEGITIMACY

Instead of treating victory simply as conquering Caerlyn, the game could track several forms of authority.

**Blood** represents dynastic legitimacy.

**Nobility** represents support from the Great Houses.

**People** represents popular support.

**Flame** represents religious legitimacy.

**Steel** represents military power.

**Coin** represents economic control.

Every claimant begins with radically different strengths.

Mira begins with Crown authority and information.

Ethan begins with reputation.

Arlo begins with almost nothing but potentially possesses extraordinary dynastic legitimacy.

Alistair begins with Steel and Coin.

The player decides how to build the rest.

---

# INFORMATION AS A WEAPON

Secrets should be as valuable as armies.

During the campaign the player might discover evidence concerning Ethan's exile, Arlo's parentage, Mira's involvement in Erol's death, Alistair's secret correspondence, corrupt nobles, hidden children, old crimes and forbidden relationships.

Information can be:

Revealed.

Destroyed.

Forged.

Sold.

Used for blackmail.

Or deliberately ignored.

Two players controlling the same claimant could therefore experience completely different political realities.

---

# THE POSSIBILITY OF ALLIANCE

None of the claimants necessarily needs to die.

Mira could marry Alistair.

Ethan could recognise Arlo.

Arlo could appoint Ethan Protector.

Mira could legitimise Arlo and attempt to rule through him.

Alistair could support Ethan in exchange for enormous northern autonomy.

Temporary alliances might also form against whoever becomes too powerful.

This allows the political map to constantly change.

Today's enemy may be tomorrow's only hope.

---

# SUPPORTING CHARACTERS

## Captain Garran Vale

Commander of the Queen's Guard.

Forty-four years old.

He owes his career to Ethan but owes his position to Mira.

An honourable man placed in a situation where honour offers no obvious answer.

He can become one of the game's most important military allies.

---

## Lady Celene Veyne

Forty-nine.

Widowed head of House Veyne.

Probably the most politically intelligent noble outside the royal family.

She has no ideological attachment to any claimant.

Her concern is preventing the kingdom from destroying itself.

Celene rarely threatens anyone.

She simply ensures that people understand what will happen if they disappoint her.

---

## Master Oren Pell

Sixty-seven.

Royal physician.

He knows exactly how sick Erol was.

He also knows what medicines were administered during the King's final night.

He refuses to tell anyone everything.

Not because he is necessarily loyal to Mira.

But because Erol made him swear an oath.

---

## Nessa Grey

Twenty-two.

Former chamber servant.

Missing.

Possibly the only surviving witness capable of establishing what happened on Erol's final night.

Every claimant wants to find her.

For very different reasons.

---

## Marshal Bren Tal

Fifty-eight.

Commander of the Royal Army.

A professional soldier with little patience for court politics.

Bren's refusal to choose sides initially prevents immediate civil war.

Eventually neutrality will become impossible.

---

## Lord Tomas Maren

Sixty-two.

The man who raised Arlo.

He knows far more about Arlo's origins than he has ever admitted.

His greatest fear is that revealing the truth will destroy the young man he regards as his son.

---

## Lady Evelyne Maren

Fifty-nine.

Arlo's adoptive mother in everything except name.

Unlike Tomas, she despises Erol.

She believes the King used their family to hide his shame.

If Arlo pursues the throne she may strongly oppose him.

---

## Edric Edevane

Thirty-six.

Mira's nephew.

Charming, ambitious and dangerously convinced of his own intelligence.

He publicly supports his aunt.

Privately he is already asking what happens if she loses.

---

# THE SHADOW OF WAR

Beyond the succession crisis, Nezrel's enemies are watching.

Across the eastern border lies **Varos**, a kingdom Erol defeated twenty years earlier.

Varos lost valuable territory in the war.

Its king has never accepted the settlement.

To the west, raiders increasingly cross the frontier.

Pirates harass southern shipping.

Nezrel therefore cannot spend years fighting itself without consequences.

As the civil conflict grows, foreign powers begin supporting different claimants.

Money arrives.

Weapons arrive.

Mercenaries arrive.

Eventually armies may arrive.

The throne gradually stops being merely a domestic dispute.

It becomes a struggle for Nezrel's survival.

---

# WHY THE TITLE "USURPER" WORKS

The most important idea is that **every playable character can legitimately be called the usurper.**

Mira's opponents say she steals her husband's crown.

Ethan's opponents say he forfeited his rights when he accepted exile.

Arlo's opponents say a bastard stable boy has no right to rule legitimate nobles.

Alistair barely pretends that he isn't taking the throne through force.

But after enough victories, alliances, betrayals and compromises, something interesting happens.

People stop saying:

**Usurper.**

They begin saying:

**Your Majesty.**

And history remembers whichever version of events the victor chooses to write.

That should be the heart of the game.

You are not simply trying to become King or Queen of Nezrel.

You are trying to make the kingdom believe that you always should have been.
